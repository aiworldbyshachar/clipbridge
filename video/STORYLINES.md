# ClipBridge — storylines

The one thing: **you can copy on your Mac and paste on your PC (and back), instantly.**
What the viewer remembers: "I'll never email a link to myself again."
The spine: one link — `docs.company.com/q3-launch-final-v7` — copied on the Mac and needed in an email on the PC.

## A. The link that couldn't cross (picked)

- **Device / form:** life before and after, told as one object across two computers; object-to-object chain.
- One desk, a Mac and a PC side by side. A link gets copied on the Mac and gets stuck at the edge of its screen; the viewer watches the tired detours (email to yourself, chat to yourself, retyping with a typo), then a bridge draws itself between the two screens and the link simply walks across. The rest of the film rides that bridge: pairing, the shared history, privacy, languages, and back to the desk where the email finally gets sent.
- **First two seconds:** the two computers on a warm desk, the Mac's screen showing the link highlighted, the PC's email draft waiting with a blinking cursor.
- **Beats:** desk → stuck → three detours → the bridge draws → ClipBridge → copy/paste both ways → one code pairs them, same Wi-Fi → shared history (search, star, copy again) → images & formatting → no cloud, encrypted, private mode → six languages → the email gets sent → end card.
- **Look:** warm oat desk and daylight for the real world; the app's own light UI (#f5f5f8 ground, white cards, #5550d8 accent) for the product; the bridge is the one accent stroke.
- **Sound:** sparse, weary piano under the old way; the groove opens when the bridge appears; clicks only on real clicks, soft key ticks on typing, one riser into the name.

## B. The clipboard's diary

- A character piece: the clipboard itself narrates its lonely life trapped in one computer, then makes a friend across the desk. Warmer and funnier, but it puts a mascot where the product's own UI should be the proof.

## C. Two keys

- Type carries the argument: giant ⌘C and Ctrl+V keycaps, cut on the beat, every feature a key pressed. Striking and fast, but colder, and it shows less of the real app.
